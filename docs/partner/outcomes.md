# Outcomes

A hackathon is worth renewing when partners can see what it produced. Decide what you'll measure before the event, collect it during, and send each partner a short report after.

## What to measure

| Outcome | How to measure it | Who cares most |
|---|---|---|
| **Prototypes** | Submissions per challenge; how many met the challenge's test | Industry partners, foundations |
| **Quality** | Judges' scores against the published criteria | Everyone |
| **Talent** | Participants by role and experience level; introductions made; interviews, internships, or hires that follow | Employers, universities |
| **Pilots and follow-ups** | Conversations, pilots, or projects that continue with a partner | Industry partners, public sector |
| **Continued building** | Projects still active 30 and 90 days later (commits, users, funding) | Startups, foundations, organizers |
| **Reach** | Registrations, attendance, sponsor workshop attendance, qualified leads | Technology partners, sponsors |
| **Learning** | Workshop attendance, participant feedback, first-time hackers | Universities, community organizers |
| **Visibility** | Stage presentations, articles, social posts, write-up views | Everyone |
| **Participant experience** | Exit survey: would you come back, would you recommend it | Organizers |

## When to collect it

1. **Registration:** role, organization, city, experience level, and optional demographics. Sponsors use this to follow up and to justify their spend.
2. **Submission:** repo, demo video, write-up, and which challenge the project answers.
3. **Judging:** scores against each challenge's criteria and test.
4. **Exit:** a QR code survey as people leave.
5. **30 and 90 days:** a short check-in with the winning and finalist teams.

## The partner report

Send each partner one page within two weeks:

- Their challenge, and how many teams took it on
- The winning and finalist projects, with links to write-ups and demos
- Who participated (roles, experience, organizations)
- Introductions or follow-ups requested, and next steps
- A quote or two from participants
- An invitation to bring a challenge again

## What it looks like in practice

At Hack Michigan 2026, every challenge had a winner, every winning team published a write-up with a demo on Kaggle, and the winners presented at Michigan Tech Week. See [After the event](../run/after.md).
