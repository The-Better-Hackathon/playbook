# Shape Your Challenge

You bring the problem. The organizer writes the brief with you, with the solution left for the teams to invent.

## What you bring

| You bring | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **A real problem** | Something your organization or community actually faces. The organizer turns it into a [challenge brief](../challenges/index.md) with you. |
| **Data or context** (if you have it) | Public, synthetic, or shareable data makes the problem tractable. Honest gaps are fine. |
| **A person for the day** | Someone to present the challenge, answer questions, and judge your challenge. |
| **Optional: a workshop or tools** | If you have a platform, credits, or expertise, a short workshop helps every team. |
| **Optional: a prize** | Cash, swag, or better yet a next step: a pilot conversation, a demo on your stage, a featured article. |

What you don't need to provide: a solution, an architecture, or a spec. The best results come from leaving that to the teams.

## How it works

| When | What happens | Your time |
|---|---|---|
| 8 to 12 weeks out | Intro call: share your problem, data, and what success would look like | About 1 hour |
| 6 to 8 weeks out | The organizer drafts the brief; you review it | About 1 hour |
| 4 to 6 weeks out | The brief is published with the rest of the challenges | None |

??? question "Do we need to provide data?"
    No, but it helps. Public or synthetic data works well, and an honest description of what's missing is fine. The DTE Energy challenge used only public and synthetic data.

??? question "Do we have to know what we want built?"
    No, and it works better if you don't. Bring the problem and what success would look like. The brief leaves the solution to the teams.
