A challenge-led hackathon gives your organization something most sponsorships don't: dozens of people working on **your** problem, in the open, for a weekend. You leave with working prototypes, new ideas, and relationships with the people who built them.

## Why bring a challenge

| Your organization | Why it's worth it |
|---|---|
| **Enterprise and industry** | Explore a real business problem at low cost and low risk. See several approaches side by side, and meet builders who already understand your domain. |
| **Startups** | Put your API, product, or data in front of builders. Get feedback, early users, and potential collaborators or hires. |
| **Universities and schools** | Give students applied, industry-connected projects with portfolio-ready outcomes, and build relationships with employers. |
| **Foundations and public sector** | Surface practical solutions to community and economic challenges, with measurable outcomes you can report. |
| **Technology partners** | Get your platform used on real problems, and learn how builders combine it with other tools. |
| **Community organizations** | Bring your community's real needs to people who can build for them. |

## What you provide

| You bring | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **A real problem** | Something your organization or community actually faces. The organizer turns it into a [challenge brief](../challenges/index.md) with you. |
| **Data or context** (if you have it) | Public, synthetic, or shareable data makes the problem tractable. Honest gaps are fine. |
| **A person for the day** | Someone to present the challenge, answer questions, and judge your challenge. |
| **Optional: a workshop or tools** | If you have a platform, credits, or expertise, a short workshop helps every team. |
| **Optional: a prize** | Cash, swag, or better yet a next step: a pilot conversation, a demo on your stage, a featured article. |

What you don't need to provide: a solution, an architecture, or a spec. The best results come from leaving that to the teams.

## How it works

| When | What happens | Your time |
|---|---|---|
| 8 to 12 weeks out | Intro call: share your problem, data, and what success would look like | About 1 hour |
| 6 to 8 weeks out | The organizer drafts the brief; you review it | About 1 hour |
| 4 to 6 weeks out | The brief is published with the rest of the challenges | None |
| Event | Present the challenge, visit teams, judge | Part of a day to the full event |
| After | Review the write-ups, meet the teams you want to follow up with | As much as you like |

## What you get back

- **Working prototypes** built against your challenge, with public write-ups and demos
- **Different approaches** to the same problem, side by side
- **Talent:** direct contact with the developers, designers, and researchers who built them
- **Qualified connections** from a registration list with role and organization
- **Visibility:** your challenge, your brand, and your winners on stage
- **A next step,** if you want one: a pilot conversation, an internship, or a follow-up project

See how to [measure and report outcomes](outcomes.md).
