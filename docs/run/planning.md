# Planning Checklist

What to settle with your organizers, sponsors, and partners before the event. These come from the planning meetings behind Hack Michigan, A2Tech360, and Michigan DevFest.

## Decide early

- [ ] **Format:** hack day, 24 hours, or 48 hours. Overnight vs. single day decides the schedule, meals, code freeze, and social events. See [Choose a format](formats/index.md).
- [ ] **Date and venue:** check for conflicts like home football weekends, and pick a venue with easy parking.
- [ ] **Name:** choose a name your own community owns, so the event can grow year over year and stays independent of any one partner's brand.
- [ ] **Anchor moment:** a tech week, conference, or summit gives you a stage for winners and a built-in audience.

## Registration and attendee data

- [ ] **Own the registration list,** or have full access to it. Waiting months for a partial list from a third party is a common failure.
- [ ] **Collect what sponsors need:** name, email, company or school, role, city, and optional demographic information. Sponsors use this to justify their spend and to follow up.
- [ ] **Test the form** before marketing starts. A required field that shouldn't be (like a dietary checkbox) can block sign-ups.
- [ ] **Registration is how provisioning works.** Sponsors provision accounts and credits from the list, so it has to be accurate and on time.

## Sponsors, tools, and provisioning

- [ ] Confirm which tools each sponsor provides and the credit or usage budget over the event days.
- [ ] Write the [hackathon guide](hackathon-guide.md) once the challenges are final, and send it before the event.
- [ ] Set up a GitHub org for the event or track, pre-install any tools (for example an AI code reviewer), and provide a template repo or [starter kit](../build/index.md).
- [ ] Decide whether any technology is required. If a host requires a framework, make sure mentors can support it alongside sponsor tools, or make an exception for a track.
- [ ] Consider small technology prizes ("best use of…") to encourage experimenting across tools.

## Workshops

- [ ] Each tech sponsor runs its own workshop. Confirm the topic and presenter.
- [ ] Workshops are open to all attendees, not only one track.
- [ ] Centralized or parallel rooms? Weekends usually have rooms to spare.
- [ ] Add How to Win, sized to your [format](formats/index.md).

## Submissions

- [ ] Agree on one submission platform with every organizer (Devpost, Kaggle, or a sponsor platform).
- [ ] If a track needs a second submission, say so clearly: same project, two places.
- [ ] Say whether a project can win a track prize and an overall prize.
- [ ] Publish exactly what to submit: repo URL, demo video, problem and solution statements, technologies used.

## Judges and prizes

See [Judging and prizes](judging-and-prizes.md).

## Communications

- [ ] Shared drive for all event materials.
- [ ] Flyers and a website with every partner's logo, before newsletters and social posts go out.
- [ ] A Discord (or similar) with announcements, help, and track channels.

## Running a track inside another event

Sometimes you host a track inside a larger hackathon instead of running your own. Settle these before committing:

- [ ] **Registration access:** you get the full list for your track, with the fields your sponsors need.
- [ ] **Track selection:** at registration or on the day?
- [ ] **What sponsorship covers:** prizes only, or event costs too?
- [ ] **Branding:** your track keeps its own name and sponsors, and the host's brand doesn't overshadow local partners.
- [ ] **Decision-making:** who plans what, and how much say your group has.
- [ ] **Talk to others who've done it.** Ask another group that ran a track in the same series what they did and didn't control.

## Planning meetings

A short recurring "reconnect" keeps everyone aligned. A standing agenda:

1. Registration and marketing status
2. Schedule and format
3. Sponsors, tools, and provisioning
4. Workshops
5. Submissions and judging
6. Prizes and budget
7. Next steps: owner and date for each
