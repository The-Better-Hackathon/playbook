# Hack Day (1 Day)

About eight hours of building in a single day. This is the format for the AI Hackathon on the Friday of Michigan DevFest, and it works well for first events, conference add-ons, and campus or corporate hack days.

## Sample schedule

| Time | Block | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 8:00 AM | Check-in and registration | Have provisioning links ready at the door |
| 9:00 AM | Welcome | Sponsors introduce themselves and their challenges |
| 9:30 AM | AI tools and provisioning overview | Team registration, where to find the challenges and tools, how to submit, what judges expect |
| 10:00 AM | **Hacking begins.** Workshops (open to all) | Sponsor workshops run while teams build. A 30-minute [How to Win](../../win/index.md) fits here |
| 12:00 PM | Lunch | |
| 1:00 PM | Office hours | Sponsors and mentors walk the room |
| 3:00 PM | Workshops continue | Short, practical sessions only |
| 6:00 PM | **Code freeze** | Organizers start pre-screening submissions |
| 7:00 PM | **Submissions due** | |
| 7:30 PM | Judging and awards | Finalists present; target wrap by about 9 PM |

## What's different about a hack day

- **Send the hackathon guide early.** There is no time to troubleshoot accounts on the day. Teams should arrive with accounts and credits working.
- **Keep challenges tight.** One or two, with a clear test a team can hit in eight hours.
- **Workshops are open to everyone.** A track is defined by its problem and its prizes, not by who can attend which session.
- **Pre-screen, then present.** Judges review submissions between code freeze and the deadline, and only the top projects present live. Tell the non-finalists so they can head home, and collect feedback with a QR code at the exit.
- **Skip the happy hour.** In a one-day format, lunch and the awards are the social moments. If the event becomes overnight, move the happy hour to the first evening.
- **Use the short How to Win.** Focus on Steps 1, 4, 5, 7, and 8 of the workbook: the idea, the scope, roles and plan, the demo video, and the checklist.

## Checklist

- [ ] Overnight vs. single day confirmed with every organizer
- [ ] Hackathon guide sent at least a week before
- [ ] Provisioning done from the registration list before the day
- [ ] Pre-screen rubric agreed with judges
- [ ] Exit QR code for feedback and follow-up
