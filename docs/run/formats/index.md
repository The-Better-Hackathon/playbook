The model works at three sizes. The pieces stay the same (co-created challenges, a tools and provisioning session, How to Win, a code freeze, practice before judging); what changes is how much time each one gets.

| | [Hack day](hack-day.md) | [24 hours](24-hour.md) | [48 hours](48-hour.md) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | One day, about 8 AM to 9 PM | Two days, overnight | Three days, two nights |
| **Hacking time** | About 8 hours | About 24 hours | About 40 to 48 hours |
| **Example** | Michigan DevFest + AI Hackathon (the Friday before the conference) | A2Tech360 Local Impact track, Hack Dearborn | Hack Michigan |
| **Best for** | Conference add-ons, first events, enterprise and partner hack days, campus hack days, people who can't stay overnight | University and community hackathons, a partner's track inside a larger event | Industry challenges that need real depth, flagship regional events |
| **Challenges** | One or two, tightly scoped | A few tracks or challenges | Several sponsor challenges |
| **Workshops** | Short and mostly before building starts | Day one, alongside building | Spread over the first two days |
| **How to Win** | 30-minute version, first thing | 45 to 60 minutes, once teams have an idea | Full 60 minutes on the morning of day two |
| **Judging** | Pre-screen, then finalists present | Every team presents, often in rooms | Every team presents; winners go to a bigger stage |
| **Social** | Lunch, and awards at the end | Dinner the first night | Happy hour, dinner, movie night, morning yoga |

## How to pick

- **Can your participants stay overnight?** If not, run a hack day. Confirm this early: it decides the schedule, meals, code freeze, and whether there's a happy hour.
- **How deep are the challenges?** A problem that needs data wrangling and a working prototype (like utility pole risk scoring) needs 24 to 48 hours. A hack day suits challenges where a strong prototype of one flow is enough.
- **Who are you inviting?** Working professionals and parents are more likely to come to a single day. Students are more likely to stay overnight.
- **Are you part of a larger event?** If you're running a track inside someone else's hackathon, match their format and read [Planning](../planning.md#running-a-track-inside-another-event) first.

## Same in every format

1. Publish challenges, rules, and judging criteria before the event.
2. Send the [hackathon guide](../hackathon-guide.md) ahead so teams arrive with accounts ready.
3. Run the tools and provisioning session before hacking starts.
4. Code freeze before the submission deadline.
5. Practice demos, or at least a pre-screen, before judging.
6. Give the winners a next step: a talk, a pilot conversation, a bigger stage.

## Using the workbook in each format

| Workbook step | Hack day | 24 hours | 48 hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Idea | In the room | In the room | In the room |
| 2. Research | Impact data and "do it better" only | In the room | In the room |
| 3. Finalize | Is it realistic? only | First four questions | All of it |
| 4. MVP | Scope only | In the room | In the room |
| 5. Hack it out | Roles and build plan | In the room | In the room |
| 6. Pitch | Tell a story | In the room | In the room |
| 7. Demo | Video script | In the room | In the room |
| 8. Submit | Checklist | In the room | In the room |
