# The Participant Experience

A hackathon isn't only its schedule. It's the experience people have from the first time they see the website to the day their project is celebrated. People remember whether they felt welcomed, included, and supported, and that comes from small, intentional details at every touchpoint.

Every team member, volunteer, mentor, partner, and sponsor is part of that experience. Walk the journey below one stage at a time, and assign an owner to each touchpoint.

=== "1. Before"

    **People should feel:** "This is for me, and I know what to expect."

    **Website**

    - [ ] Says who it's for in the first line, including beginners if they're welcome
    - [ ] Shows the date, place, format, cost (ideally free), and whether it's overnight
    - [ ] Shows real faces and past projects, not only sponsor logos
    - [ ] States the Code of Conduct and accessibility information

    **Registration**

    - [ ] Short: name, email, organization, role, city, experience level
    - [ ] Optional fields stay optional: pronouns, dietary needs, accessibility needs, demographics
    - [ ] Test the form before marketing starts, on a phone
    - [ ] Confirmation email right away, with the date, place, and a calendar invite

    **The week before**

    - [ ] Send the [hackathon guide](hackathon-guide.md) so people arrive with accounts ready
    - [ ] Tell people what to bring, where to park or which bus to take, and which door to use
    - [ ] Invite them to the Discord (or similar) so they can meet teammates early
    - [ ] Say what's provided: meals, power, Wi-Fi, a quiet room, and a place to sleep if overnight

=== "2. Arrival"

    **People should feel:** "I'm expected, and I belong here."

    **Check-in**

    - [ ] A greeter at the door who says hello before asking for a name
    - [ ] Clear signs from the parking lot or bus stop to the room
    - [ ] Fast check-in: a QR code or a short line, never a long wait
    - [ ] Name tags with space for pronouns
    - [ ] Wi-Fi details, the schedule, and the Discord link posted where everyone can see them

    **The first hour**

    - [ ] Breakfast and coffee ready when doors open
    - [ ] A clear welcome that explains the day in plain language
    - [ ] Help for people without a team: a team-formation session, and a volunteer who introduces people
    - [ ] Say out loud where to get help and how to reach the Code of Conduct contact

=== "3. Building"

    **People should feel:** "I have what I need, and someone will help if I get stuck."

    **Food and drink**

    - [ ] Label every dish, including allergens
    - [ ] Vegetarian, vegan, halal, and gluten-free options, planned from registration answers
    - [ ] Water, coffee, and snacks available the whole time, not only at meals

    **The space**

    - [ ] Enough outlets and power strips at every table
    - [ ] Wi-Fi tested with the expected number of people
    - [ ] A quiet or low-sensory room, a prayer or reflection space, and a lactation room
    - [ ] Accessible entrances, restrooms, and seating; gender-neutral restrooms
    - [ ] Captions or slides shared for every workshop

    **Help**

    - [ ] A simple way to ask for a mentor (a channel, a form, or a help desk)
    - [ ] Office hours posted, with sponsors and mentors walking the room
    - [ ] Regular, calm announcements: what's next and how much time is left

    **Overnight (24- and 48-hour events)**

    - [ ] A safe, comfortable place to rest, with dimmed lights and quiet hours
    - [ ] Security and a staffed contact through the night
    - [ ] Safe walking routes, lighting, and parking information for anyone leaving late
    - [ ] Late-night food, and breakfast in the morning

=== "4. Finishing"

    **People should feel:** "I know exactly what to do, and my work was seen."

    **Submission**

    - [ ] Share the submission checklist on day one, not an hour before the deadline
    - [ ] A draft checkpoint before the final deadline
    - [ ] A help desk for submission problems in the final hours
    - [ ] Confirm each submission was received

    **Judging**

    - [ ] Tell every team when and where they present, and for how long
    - [ ] Keep to time so the last team gets the same attention as the first
    - [ ] Judges ask questions with curiosity and thank each team
    - [ ] Written feedback for every team, not only finalists

    **Awards**

    - [ ] Recognize every team, not only the winners (a slideshow of all projects works well)
    - [ ] Say why each winner won
    - [ ] Photos of every team and every winner
    - [ ] End on time, with what's next

=== "5. After"

    **People should feel:** "That mattered, and there's a place for me to keep going."

    **Within 24 hours**

    - [ ] Thank-you email with photos, the winners, and a short feedback survey
    - [ ] Thank volunteers, mentors, judges, and partners by name

    **Within two weeks**

    - [ ] Celebrate the winners publicly: posts, write-ups, and a stage if you have one
    - [ ] Send each partner their [report](../partner/outcomes.md)
    - [ ] Share what you learned from the feedback, and what you'll change

    **30 and 90 days**

    - [ ] Check in with finalist and winning teams
    - [ ] Invite everyone to the next event, a pop-up, or the community

## Who owns what

| Role | Owns |
|---|---|
| Lead organizer | The whole journey, and the decisions |
| Welcome lead | Website copy, registration, confirmation emails, check-in |
| Experience lead | Food, space, accessibility, overnight comfort and safety |
| Mentor coordinator | Mentors, office hours, and the help channel |
| Code of Conduct contact | Reports and support, reachable the whole event |
| Judging lead | Submission, judging schedule, feedback |
| Community lead | Photos, social, follow-up, and celebrating winners |
| Every volunteer, partner, and sponsor | Being welcoming in every interaction |

Small teams can combine roles. What matters is that every touchpoint has a name next to it.
