# Michigan Small Business AI Adoption

**The AI Collective Detroit** · Hack Michigan 2026 · May 15–17 · TechTown Detroit

*Build something a Michigan small business owner without a tech team can actually deploy in 30 days, for under $50 a month.*

## 1. The problem

Most AI tooling assumes the user has an engineer in the building. Most Michigan small businesses don't: restaurants, salons, contractors, retail shops, service businesses, and nonprofits. These owners are technically literate enough to run a business (point-of-sale, accounting software, social media, scheduling), but they aren't going to write code, hire an ML engineer, or wire together a multi-tool pipeline.

Most AI products assume per-seat enterprise budgets, someone who can read API docs, and comfort with abstraction. A small business owner who reads about generative AI in the news has very few realistic paths from "this seems useful" to "this is running in my business and making me money."

This is an access equity problem as much as a product problem. The AI Collective Detroit works alongside TechTown, Bamboo, and a partner network that already serves this audience. The distribution exists. What's missing is the product.

!!! quote "Key insight"
    The businesses that could benefit most from AI are also the ones least likely to adopt it. This challenge is about closing that gap.

## 2. Context: what "deployable" means

Three hard constraints. Each one rules out a category of solution that wouldn't reach a small business owner.

- **No engineering staff:** setup, integration, and maintenance must be doable by someone whose job is running a business.
- **Under $50 per month:** total operating cost. This forces creative use of free tiers, off-the-shelf infrastructure, and efficient model choices.
- **Deployable in 30 days:** from discovering the tool to having it working in the business.

**Who the owners are:** real operations, often single-location and family-owned, revenue from $50K to a few million. They use Square, Toast, Shopify, QuickBooks, Mailchimp, Instagram, and Google Business Profile. They lack time, not ambition.

## 3. What good looks like

1. **It solves a problem the owner can name:** a specific, repeated pain (booking, customer messages, inventory, paperwork) that already costs time or money.
2. **The owner can set it up themselves:** no installation calls, no developer onboarding, no SQL.
3. **It runs without ongoing attention:** no daily thinking about prompts, models, or integrations.
4. **It pays for itself:** at under $50 a month, the savings in time, recovered customers, or avoided mistakes are obvious.
5. **It's discoverable through trusted channels:** something TechTown, Bamboo, or a similar partner could recommend.

!!! success "The test"
    If a Michigan small business owner finds your tool tomorrow, can they have it running, and earning back its cost, within a month? Not "is the AI impressive?" Not "does the demo look good?" Can a real person with no tech team actually use it?

## 4. Data and resources

Teams choose a specific small business type and design around the tools that owners already use. Off-the-shelf tooling and free tiers are encouraged.

## 5. Evaluation and constraints

| Criterion | What judges look for |
|---|---|
| Access equity | Genuinely usable by owners without engineering staff |
| Deployability | A realistic 30-day path from sign-up to working |
| Cost fit | Under $50/month at typical small-business scale |
| Real pain | A problem an owner would pay to remove |
| Partner fit | Plausible distribution through TechTown, Bamboo, and similar networks |
| Sustained use | Built for daily operation, not a one-time demo |

**Constraints:** works without engineering staff · under $50/month total · 30-day deployment window · built for Michigan small businesses · honest about what it can and can't do for a non-technical user.

## 6. Directions worth exploring

Multi-language onboarding (Spanish and Arabic for metro Detroit) · templated setup for the top three business types · light analytics that show the owner the dollar value created · partner co-branding hooks.

## 7. The key question

**Can you build something a Michigan small business owner, with no tech team and a $50/month budget, actually deploys and uses for the next year?** If yes, you've moved AI access equity forward where it matters most. The product is yours to invent.
