# Judging and Prizes

Judging should reward the thing the challenge asked for. In this model, each challenge brief publishes its own criteria, and every team presents against them.

## How judging fits together

1. **Each challenge has its own criteria.** Four to six, drawn from the brief. See the [examples](../challenges/index.md#hack-michigan-2026-challenges).
2. **Every brief ends with one test.** Judges apply it to every demo in that challenge.
3. **Presentations have a fixed shape.** For example, 5 minutes: 3 to 4 minutes presenting and 1 to 2 minutes of video demo.
4. **Write-ups carry the demo.** Teams publish a write-up with the presentation and demo, so judges can review before and after the live session.

## Judging by format

| | Hack day | 24 hours | 48 hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who presents | Finalists only, after a pre-screen | Every team, often in parallel rooms | Every team, then winners on a bigger stage |
| Pre-screen | Between code freeze and the deadline | From the morning draft checkpoint | During practice demos |
| Judges | 6 or 7 | Up to 20 for a large event | Enough for every challenge |

## Judges

- **Use an odd number** per panel so there are no ties.
- **Mix roles:** engineers, designers, product managers, and executives.
- **Workshop facilitators make natural judges.** They know the tools and have met the teams.
- **Prize sponsors judge their own prize.**
- **Give judges one page** with the brief, the criteria, and the test.
- **Keep a shared nominations list** so organizers can fill gaps early.

## Event-wide criteria (Hack Michigan)

| Criterion | What judges look for |
|---|---|
| Practical application | Meaningful use of AI to solve a real challenge |
| Local impact | Creates or retains opportunity for workers, businesses, and communities |
| Business value | Clear, measurable impact on productivity, efficiency, cost, or innovation |
| Implementability | A working prototype that could realistically be deployed |
| Storytelling | A clear, memorable story about the user and the change |

## Prizes

- **Don't announce amounts ahead of time.** Announce the categories, and keep the focus on the problem.
- **Keep cash modest for a first event.** A few hundred dollars still matters to a solo participant.
- **Add non-cash prizes:** gift cards, tablets, dev boards, and exclusive swag.
- **Swag goes a long way.** Winner jackets, t-shirts, tumblers, stickers, and bags; a track can have its own shirt with sponsor logos.
- **The best prizes are next steps:** a main-stage demo, a featured article, a pilot conversation with the sponsor.
- **One winner per track,** plus overall 1st, 2nd, and 3rd if you run the whole event.
- **Community favorite:** let everyone vote. It keeps people engaged through judging.

## Tips for organizers

- Publish criteria when you publish the challenges, not on judging day.
- Hold practice demos (or a pre-screen) before judging.
- Point teams to the judging criteria map in the [team workbook](../participate/workbook.md) so they design toward the rubric.
