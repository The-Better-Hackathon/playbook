# Run of Show and Staffing

The schedule tells participants what happens. The run of show tells your team who makes it happen, where, and when. Write one document with both, and walk through it together at the venue before doors open.

## The run of show

Start from your format's sample schedule ([hack day](formats/hack-day.md), [24 hours](formats/24-hour.md), [48 hours](formats/48-hour.md)) and add a row for every setup, handoff, and cue.

| Time | What happens | Where | Owner | Cues and notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7:00 AM | Setup: signs, power strips, check-in table | Main room | Logistics lead | Painter's tape only on painted walls |
| 7:30 AM | Partners arrive and set up | Partner area | Partner lead | Wi-Fi details and table numbers ready |
| 8:00 AM | Check-in opens | Lobby | Check-in lead | Walk-ins wait until pre-registered people are through |
| 9:00 AM | Welcome | Main stage | Emcee | Slides loaded, mic tested, partners in the front row |

- **Leave buffer** between blocks. Things run late, especially judging.
- **Ask someone who has run an event** to read it for realistic timing.
- **Keep it in one shared place,** and post changes to the team channel.

## Roles

One person owns each role. On small teams, leads hold more than one.

| Role | Owns |
|---|---|
| **Lead organizer** | Decisions, the emcee or the person who picks one, and the emergency plan |
| **Floater** | Walks the event all day, spots problems, and fixes them. No other duties |
| **Help desk** | Questions, lost items, announcements, and the first stop for any issue |
| **Check-in** | The line, name tags, late registrations, and consent forms |
| **Food** | Deliveries, dietary meals, serving lines, and cleanup |
| **Partners** | Partner arrival, setup, workshops, and keeping partners in the room |
| **Mentors** | Mentor schedule, the mentor desk, and matching teams who are stuck |
| **Judging** | Judge briefing, rooms or tables, scoring, and results |
| **A/V and stage** | Mics, slides, streaming, and the main stage |
| **Comms** | Discord, social posts, and fast updates when the schedule changes |

**Volunteers get named tasks,** not general duties, on shifts of a few hours. Give each one a short note: where to be, what to do, and who to ask.

## Before doors open

- [ ] **Walk through the run of show at the venue** with every lead, the day before if you can
- [ ] **Signs** for check-in, restrooms, food, workshops, the help desk, the rest area, and staff-only spaces, with copies for doors and elevators
- [ ] **Name tags or lanyards in a color per role:** participants, organizers, volunteers, mentors, judges, partners. Print extra blanks
- [ ] **Power at every table,** cords taped down, Wi-Fi tested at full load
- [ ] **Judging tables or rooms** numbered, with extra table numbers

## Check-in

- **A simple list and a checkbox** is enough. Check ID only if your event requires it.
- **Put the name tag station away from the line,** so it doesn't slow check-in.
- **Take walk-ins on a separate form** after pre-registered people are through.
- **Hand out swag or stickers in line** to keep it friendly.
- **Have provisioning links ready at the door,** so accounts work before the welcome.

## The help desk

- **Staff it for the whole event,** including overnight.
- **Make it the place for announcements:** what just happened, what's next, and where. Expect a wave of questions after each one.
- **Use roles in Discord** (organizer, mentor, partner, participant) so people know who to ask.

### Discord channels to start with

| Channel | For |
|---|---|
| announcements | Organizers only |
| ask-organizers | Logistics questions |
| ask-mentors | Technical help, with an @mentor role |
| team-formation | Finding teammates |
| introductions | Organizers post first to set the tone |
| one per track or challenge | Questions for that partner |
| one per partner tool | Workshops and tool help |
| mentors-internal, organizers-internal | Private coordination |

## Mentors

- **Recruit** teaching assistants, alumni, and partner engineers first. They already like helping.
- **Use a sign-up** with time slots, and invite mentors to stay longer.
- **Give mentors one page:** how to help without taking over, the tools and starter kit, and good questions to ask a stuck team.
- **Staff a mentor desk** in person, and watch the ask-mentors channel online.

## Opening and closing

**The welcome** sets the energy for the whole event. Keep partner segments short and coach partners ahead of time.

1. Who the organizers are and how to reach them
2. Wi-Fi, Discord, the room layout, food, and the rest area
3. The challenges, presented by the partners who wrote them
4. The schedule, with the deadlines repeated
5. The rules, judging, and the Code of Conduct

**The awards** are what people remember. Get the winners list from each partner before it starts, warn everyone 15 minutes ahead, and celebrate what every team built, not only the winners.

## Submission deadline

Make submitting easy to get right: every team member registered on the submission platform before the event, a code freeze before the deadline, and organizers checking which teams haven't submitted yet. See [Submit and demo](../participate/submit.md).
