# Hackathon Guide Template

The hackathon guide is the one document every participant needs before hacking starts. Publish it a week or two ahead and walk through it in the tools and provisioning workshop.

## What goes in it

1. **The challenge.** Link to each challenge brief, or state the theme with a few example use cases.
2. **A note on data.** The rules below, word for word.
3. **The tools.** For each sponsor tool: what it is, how to get access, and one or two ways teams can use it. Say plainly that no single tool is required unless your rules say so.
4. **Provisioning steps.** Account sign-up, team or cloud account invites, credits, and how to confirm it worked.
5. **Limits.** Credit amounts, trial limits, and what happens when they run out. Tell teams to coordinate who is signed in so they don't double-spend.
6. **Out of scope.** Features or services not available for the event.
7. **Save your work.** Accounts often end with the event. Explain how to export before they do.
8. **Learning resources.** Short videos, labs, and docs, with approximate times.

## Data rules

Participants bring their own data to build their solution. As you collect it:

- Teams are responsible for making sure data is compliant.
- Data from public websites may be used if the terms allow it. Keep a list of the sites you use.
- Do not use confidential company data, or any data without the owner's permission.
- Do not use client data.
- Do not use data containing personal information.
- Do not use data taken from social media.

## Tips worth including

- Use your first AI prompts to test the idea before you build on it.
- Track credit usage so you don't run out at a critical moment.
- Keep API keys out of your repo.
- Save useful prompts and outputs locally before trials expire.

## Examples

The Hack Michigan and A2Tech360 guides followed this structure, featuring IBM Bob, watsonx.ai, watsonx Orchestrate, and IBM Granite, alongside Google Cloud credits.
