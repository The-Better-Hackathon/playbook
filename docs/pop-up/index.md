# Pop-Up Hack Day

A free, low-barrier meetup in a public space, usually 4 to 8 hours. People bring whatever they're working on, build alongside each other, and demo their progress at the end of the day. No challenge brief, no judging, no pressure.

In Detroit, pop-ups happen in places that belong to everyone: **Campus Martius**, **Michigan Central**, the **Detroit Institute of Arts**, and the **Detroit Public Library**. Hacking in public spaces is a way to reclaim the city's spaces for its community, and to show people walking by that technology is built here, by people like them.

## Why run one

- **Lowest barrier to entry.** No team, idea, or experience required. Come for an hour or the whole day.
- **Keeps the community connected** between larger events.
- **A first step for newcomers** who aren't ready for a 24- or 48-hour hackathon.
- **Visible in the city.** Passersby see builders at work and can join in.

## Sample schedule

| Time | Block | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 10:00 AM | Arrive and set up | Greeters at the entrance; sign-in with a QR code |
| 10:30 AM | Welcome circle | Everyone says their name and what they're working on in one sentence |
| 11:00 AM | **Design for AI** workshop (open to all) | 30 minutes: start from a person and their problem, not the model |
| 11:30 AM | Build | Organizers and volunteers walk the room and connect people who can help each other |
| 12:30 PM | Lunch | Keep it simple and label what's in it |
| 1:30 PM | Build | Optional short lightning talk or skill share |
| 3:30 PM | Show and tell | Anyone who wants to demos their progress in two or three minutes |
| 4:30 PM | Wrap up | Group photo, the date of the next pop-up, and a feedback QR code |

## What to bring (tell people in advance)

- A laptop and charger
- A project you're working on, or an open mind to join someone else's
- Headphones, and a water bottle

## Organizer checklist

- [ ] **Confirm the space** with the venue: tables, power outlets, Wi-Fi, restrooms, accessibility, and any permit or reservation it needs
- [ ] **Bring power strips and extension cords.** Outlets are always the bottleneck in public spaces.
- [ ] **Have a Wi-Fi backup,** like a hotspot, in case the public network is slow
- [ ] **Make it easy to find you:** a sign at the entrance, and a banner or table cover at your spot
- [ ] **Assign a greeter** for the first hour, and one person to connect newcomers with others
- [ ] **Share a Code of Conduct** and a way to reach an organizer
- [ ] **Post the next date** before people leave

## How it differs from a hack day

| | Pop-up | [Hack day](../library/formats/hack-day.md) |
|---|---|---|
| Projects | Your own | Built for a challenge |
| Teams | Optional | Teams of 1 to 5 |
| Judging and prizes | None | Pre-screen, finalists, prizes |
| Setup for participants | None | Accounts, credits, and tools |
| Where | Public spaces around the city | A venue or campus |
